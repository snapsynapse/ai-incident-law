                                   3         The court denies the motion. A party seeking a protective order must show specific prejudice or

                                   4   harm. Workday’s showing is that it is busy: it must oppose certification and move to decertify by

                                   5   November 10 while taking depositions and preparing expert reports. But the discovery at issue is its

                                   8   The plaintiffs have similar briefing burdens, and the court has held them to their discovery

                                   9   obligations. The request to defer Mr. Dahan’s deposition is moot because the plaintiffs have agreed

                                  10   not to take it before November 10.

                                   1                                                ANALYSIS

                                   2         The court may, for good cause, issue an order to protect a party from undue burden or expense,

                                   3   including by forbidding discovery or by specifying its terms, “including time.” Fed. R. Civ. P.

                                   4   26(c)(1)(A)–(B). The party seeking protection must show that “specific prejudice or harm will

                                   5   result if no protective order is granted.” Phillips ex rel. Estates of Byrd v. Gen. Motors Corp., 307

                                   6   F.3d 1206, 1210–11 (9th Cir. 2002). The court must limit discovery if the burden outweighs its

                                   7   likely benefit. Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(2)(C)(iii). The question is whether Workday has shown that

                                   8   responding to the October 9 letter before November 10 is an undue burden.

                                   9

                                  10   1. Workday Has Not Shown Good Cause to Defer Its Response to the October 9 Letter

                                  11         Workday has not made that showing, for three reasons.

                                  12         First, the burden is modest and is Workday’s regardless of the briefing schedule. The October



 United States District Court
                                  13   9 letter is a meet-and-confer letter about requests served in June 2026 and December 2025. The

                                  14   court’s standing order requires the parties to confer before they bring a dispute to the court, and

                                  15   answering the letter is that conferral. Workday answered the plaintiffs’ August 31 letter about the



Northern District of California
                                  16   HiredScore Addendum in four pages on September 7, and it can answer the October 9 letter the

                                  17   same way. To the extent the letter asks for customer data that the court has held Workday does not

                                  18   possess, Workday can say so in a sentence, as it did in September. 20 Part of what the letter seeks,

                                  19   moreover, is production that Workday promised on August 4 and that its counsel said on October

                                  20   16 was not complete. A party’s obligation to finish a production it has promised does not pause

                                  21   while the party briefs a motion.

                                  22         Second, the burden Workday identifies is the briefing window, and that burden falls on both

                                  23   sides. The plaintiffs are producing opt-in discovery every two weeks through November 30 and

                                  24   presented fifteen declarants for deposition in the second half of October, all while preparing to

                                  25   answer Workday’s opposition and decertification motion. The court imposed those obligations

                                  26   with the briefing schedule in mind. Workday itself told the plaintiffs in August that discovery is

                                   2   nine lawyers of record. The work of answering a letter and completing a promised production

                                   3   during the same window is not an undue burden.

                                   4      Third, the timing is not the plaintiffs’ doing. Workday’s argument that the plaintiffs had three

                                   5   years to take this discovery and waited until after filing their motion describes the age of the case,

                                   6   not the age of the requests. The plaintiffs served the Third Set on June 12, 2026, and raised five of

                                   7   the six categories in letters between August 21 and 31, before the September 14 motion. The sixth

                                   8   (category three) concerns records that Workday first disclosed in interrogatory answers verified

                                   9   August 24. Workday produced two-thirds of its pages after July 31, including the first HiredScore

                                  10   Addendum on August 21 and contracts through September 18. The October 9 letter followed the

                                  11   production, not the motion.

                                  12      Workday’s authorities do not change the result. Committee for Immigrant Rights and In re



 United States District Court
                                  13   Hayes stayed discovery pending motions that could have ended the case: a motion to dismiss

                                  14   raising immunity and jurisdiction, and a summary-judgment motion. Comm. for Immigrant Rts. v.

                                  15   County of Sonoma, No. CV 08-4220 PJH, 2009 WL 10692620, at *2–3 (N.D. Cal. Apr. 20, 2009);



Northern District of California
                                  16   In re Hayes, No. C10-5116BHS, 2010 WL 11685131, at *4 (W.D. Wash. Aug. 25, 2010), aff’d,

                                  17   465 F. App’x 684 (9th Cir. 2012). A stay in that setting can avoid discovery that may never be

                                  18   needed. Class certification is not dispositive: whatever the district judge decides, the case and its

                                  19   discovery continue. Ellis holds that a party moving for class certification must present its evidence

                                  20   in the opening motion, not on reply. Ellis v. J.P. Morgan Chase & Co., No. 12-cv-03897-YGR,

                                  21   2015 WL 9178076, at *5 (N.D. Cal. Dec. 17, 2015). Whether the plaintiffs may use any of this

                                  22   discovery on reply is for the district judge. Their opposition to decertification is not due until

                                  23   January 6, and nothing in Ellis limits what they may use for that.

                                  24      Also, pragmatically, denying the stay compels nothing. It leaves the parties where the rules

                                  25   and the court’s standing order put them. And under the court’s letter-brief process, an opposition is

                                  26   due five business days after the opening brief and a reply three business days after that, so no

                                   5   2. The Request to Defer the Dahan Deposition Is Moot

                                   6         Workday asked the court to defer Mr. Dahan’s deposition until it files its briefs on November

                                   7   10. On October 16, the plaintiffs agreed not to take the deposition before then. The plaintiffs’

                                   8   concession gives Workday the relief it sought, and the request is moot.

                                   9         The parties disagree about more than timing: Workday says that Mr. Dahan can be deposed

                                  10   only under the Hague Convention and has not committed to produce him after November 10, and

                                  11   the plaintiffs say that he is a managing agent whom Workday must produce. Neither side has

                                  12   asked the court to decide that dispute, and the court does not decide it. If it ripens after the parties



 United States District Court
                                  13   confer, it can be raised through the standing order’s letter-brief process.
